The 2025 Statistical Report presents WOCCU’s best available 
picture of the global credit union and financial cooperative 
movement. This year’s report captures approximately  
420 million members across 93 countries and nearly  
US$4.0 trillion in assets – demonstrating the extraordinary 
scale of cooperative finance and its important role in  
financial systems around the world.
The report draws on data from national and regional credit 
union associations, regulators, central banks and other 
authoritative sources. Through WOCCU’s global network 
of 85 national and regional member organizations, we are 
also strengthening the relationships and data infrastructure 
needed to improve the quality, consistency and usefulness 
of global credit union statistics over time.
Our work is driven by three core commitments:
AMPLIFY ADVOCACY
Elevating member voices and strengthening influence in global 
policy and regulatory conversations.
DELIVER PREMIER MEMBERS SERVICES
Expanding education, connection, and shared learning to create 
more continuous engagement across the movement.
DEMONSTRATE IMPACT AND 
THE COOPERATIVE DIFFERENCE
Producing stronger evidence, insights and storytelling that show 
the value credit unions bring to members and communities.

To build that clearer picture, this year’s report reflects a 
significant effort to strengthen the underlying methodology of 
the Statistical Report. WOCCU reviewed country-level sources 
to identify the most authoritative and current “source of truth” 
available for each market. Where credible data permitted, 
reporting was expanded beyond affiliated institutions to capture 
the full national credit union or financial cooperative system.
This approach also required greater discipline about what is 
included. Outdated or unverifiable datasets were removed, 
known limitations were documented and methodological 
inconsistencies were corrected. As a result, changes in country 
or global totals should not always be interpreted as growth or 
contraction. In some cases, they reflect improved classification, 
broader national coverage or the removal of institutions that do 
not fit WOCCU’s working definition of a credit union or bona fide 
financial cooperative.
WOCCU has also begun mapping the data ecosystems that 
support credit union reporting across its global network. Preliminary 
research reveals considerable variation in who collects the data, 
how much of the system is covered, the level of detail reported and 
whether the information is publicly accessible. In some countries, 
standardized regulatory reporting provides comprehensive system-
wide information; in others, data remain divided among regulators, 
cooperative authorities and national associations or cover only 
affiliated institutions.
The result is a report that prioritizes consistency and credibility 
over the simple accumulation of institutions and assets. This 
work is producing a clearer picture of the global movement while 
helping WOCCU identify authoritative national sources, strengthen 
partnerships with data providers and recognize where stronger data 
systems are still needed.
