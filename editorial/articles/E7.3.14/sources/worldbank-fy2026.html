International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID)

The International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) supports cross-border investment by offering an independent, specialized forum for the resolution of disputes. Effective, impartial dispute resolution strengthens confidence among investors and States and enhances predictability—core pillars of a favorable investment landscape. 

ICSID is a unique institution, created by the Convention on the Settlement of Investment Disputes between States and Nationals of Other States. This multilateral agreement, also known as the ICSID Convention, was formulated in 1965 by the Executive Directors of the World Bank.

ICSID is essential to the World Bank Group's work to accelerate job creation at scale, a strategy focused on investing in the physical and human infrastructure that people and businesses need to thrive, supporting business-enabling environments, and mobilizing private capital.

By providing an effective, impartial forum for resolving international investment disputes, ICSID mitigates perceived risk and encourages the mobilization of private capital across borders. The availability of ICSID to investors and States fosters trust and predictability, helping create investment environments that drive economic growth and job creation.

At the core of this work are ICSID's dispute administration services, which cover a range of procedures including arbitration, conciliation, mediation, and fact-finding for disputes arising from investment contracts, international treaties, and domestic investment laws. ICSID's rules and procedures are widely regarded as the gold standard for investment dispute settlement, offering a stable and predictable legal framework that balances the interests of both States and investors and has been modernized over time to reflect best practices in the field.

ICSID also supports the development of international investment law and dispute settlement by producing and sharing knowledge, building capacity, and engaging with Member States and other stakeholders on investment dispute prevention, management, and settlement.

Fiscal 2026 saw meaningful progress across all these areas, as ICSID advanced its priorities to deepen engagement and knowledge, expand mediation and amicable settlement, and uphold the highest standards in case management.

Membership Updates and Benefits
On March 6, 2026, President Nasry Juan Asfura of Honduras signed the ICSID Convention in Washington, D.C., making Honduras the 166th Signatory State to the Convention. ICSID Membership has steadily expanded since the ICSID Convention opened for signature in 1965. By the early 2000s, 149 States had signed the ICSID Convention, and 132 of those had ratified it.  By June 30, 2026, the number of signatories had increased to 166 States, with 158 of them becoming Contracting States.

ICSID membership is considered a positive factor in MIGA’s risk assessment for insurance guarantees and the World Bank Group’s overall assessment of countries’ investment landscape. ICSID membership supports States’ policies to attract and retain foreign direct investment to create more job opportunities and economic growth. It signals States’ openness to resolving disputes through arbitration, conciliation, and mediation in a trusted forum established under an international convention. There is no cost to join or maintain membership in ICSID.

Reaching Further, Working Closer

ICSID continued to deepen its worldwide engagement in fiscal 2026. Staff participated in over 180 activities, the most in a single year to date, across 60 cities as part of a robust outreach and capacity-building program. Delivered in person and remotely in the Centre’s three official languages (English, Spanish, and French), as well as in Italian and Japanese, these activities reached officials from over 90 States, along with legal practitioners, students, and representatives of regional and local institutions.

Collaboration with sister institutions also grew stronger, reinforcing a One World Bank Group approach in practice. The Centre deepened ties with country offices through targeted training sessions and worked alongside World Bank Group legal teams to engage Attorneys General and Ministers of Justice at high-level forums on business-enabling environments and public-private partnerships. Collaboration with IFC and MIGA is ongoing, focused on sharing knowledge about investor-State dispute prevention and resolution with their clients. This partnership also extended to joint analytical and operational work: World Bank Group research is examining the impact of ICSID membership on bilateral foreign direct investment flows; the Business Ready (B-READY) team integrated ICSID-related questions into its 2026 data collection cycle; and ICSID contributed to World Bank Group reports on dispute resolution, supported the development of legal frameworks for carbon markets, and co-organized a conference on investment arbitration.

ICSID also remains actively engaged in multilateral efforts to modernize investor-State dispute settlement (ISDS) mechanisms. In fiscal 2026, the Centre participated in five sessions of the United Nations Commission on International Trade Law (UNCITRAL) Working Group III, contributing substantively to State-led reform discussions, including by submitting comments on the feasibility of modifying the ICSID Convention to allow awards to be reviewed by a permanent appellate tribunal. ICSID also deepened its engagement with the International Institute for the Unification of Private Law (UNIDROIT) in support of efforts to modernize and harmonize investor-State investment contracts.

Building Capacity in Mediation and Amicable Settlement
Working with partners around the world, ICSID delivered an extensive capacity-building and knowledge-sharing program on investment mediation and amicable settlement, reaching government officials, investment promotion agencies, legal practitioners, law students, and arbitration professionals in Asia, the Middle East, Africa, Europe, the Americas, and the Pacific. This initiative spanned 50 events, including 21 capacity-building sessions delivered for State officials in response to requests from Member States and in collaboration with regional partners, reflecting a sustained institutional commitment to advancing mediation as a credible and practical tool for managing and amicably resolving investment disputes. A central pillar of this outreach was the Second Investment Mediation Insights Webinar Series, which ran from October 2025 through June 2026 across nine sessions, covering the full arc of investment mediation practice — from foundational content on the ICSID mediation process and policy landscape, to practitioner perspectives from States and investors, real-world case studies from Latin America and Europe, and practical pathways to settlement including negotiation techniques and domestic dispute management frameworks drawing on World Bank Group and UNCITRAL toolkits.

Expanding Global Presence
ICSID has long maintained a broad international reach, and expanding its global presence is among its current institutional priorities. Fiscal year 2026 marked a landmark moment in this effort with the announcement of new offices in Singapore and Paris, the Centre’s first staffed offices outside Washington, D.C. This expansion will enhance ICSID’s capacity to deliver tailored support, strengthen outreach, and maintain the highest standards in case management.

The Singapore office, announced in August 2025, is located at the World Bank Group’s Singapore office and at Maxwell Chambers. It will serve as a regional hub for capacity building in investment dispute settlement; promote the appointment of counsel, arbitrators, conciliators, and mediators from across Asia and the Pacific; and support the growing interest in mediation as a flexible and efficient approach to resolving investment disputes. The office will also enable hearings to be held closer to the parties involved, reducing time and costs.

The Paris Office, announced in March 2026 and to be located at the World Bank Group’s Paris office, will support training activities and strengthen connections with State officials, legal practitioners, and organizations in the region and beyond. Paris has long been one of the Centre’s most active hearing venues. In fiscal 2026, 30 percent of all in-person hearings were held at the World Bank Group Paris Conference Centre, ICSID’s premier hearing venue in use since 1972, and at facilities of leading institutions with which ICSID has established cooperation agreements.

Together, these offices reflect ICSID’s commitment to bringing its services closer to Member States, parties, and practitioners.

Sharing Knowledge, Supporting Practitioners and Policymakers
As part of the Knowledge Bank, ICSID produces and shares knowledge that actively contributes to the development and modernization of international investment law and dispute settlement.

In fiscal 2026, ICSID published its first in-depth study on contract-based investment dispute resolution, drawing on an analysis of 175 cases registered under the ICSID Convention and Additional Facility. The background paper examines how contract-based dispute resolution has worked in practice at ICSID, serving as both an analytical guide and practical reference for investors, States, and State entities. Key findings show that contract-based arbitrations at ICSID are more time-efficient than the overall caseload, averaging 40 months from registration to conclusion compared to 47, and settle or discontinue at a higher rate, 43 percent versus 34 percent. The paper is accompanied by a special issue of the ICSID Caseload – Statistics featuring data on contract-based cases.

A revised set of Model Clauses for Contracts was also released, providing ready-to-use drafting language for contracts between foreign investors and States or Regional Economic Integration Organizations, including State subdivisions and agencies, that seek to submit disputes to ICSID. Updated for the first time since 1993, the Model Clauses cover arbitration, conciliation, mediation, and fact-finding at ICSID, offering practical templates that parties can adapt to their specific needs.

Three issues of the ICSID Review–Foreign Investment Law Journal were published during the year. A specialized, peer-reviewed publication produced in collaboration with Oxford University Press, the journal offers analysis of legal developments, significant cases, and practical issues in foreign investment law and international dispute settlement. One issue was a special edition on the energy transition, exploring diverse perspectives on its implications for investment law.

Two issues of the ICSID Caseload – Statistics were also released. Published every six months in English, French, and Spanish, this publication offers a comprehensive, data-driven overview of all cases registered and administered by ICSID since 1972, serving as a key resource for practitioners and policymakers to track trends and developments in international investment dispute settlement.

Visit icsid.worldbank.org/resources/publications to download and read all ICSID publications.

Case Management and Dispute Resolution

ICSID administers investment disputes arising from contracts, treaties, or laws when agreed upon by the parties involved. Disputes are decided by an arbitral tribunal or a conciliation commission, whose members are typically appointed by the parties from among international jurists worldwide. The Centre also offers mediation and fact-finding procedures, and provides administrative services for arbitrations conducted under UNCITRAL and other non-ICSID frameworks.

Fiscal 2026 was a record year for ICSID. The Centre administered 363 cases—the highest in its history—up from 347 in fiscal 2025 and 341 in fiscal 2024. In addition, ICSID provided services in 15 cases governed by non-ICSID rules in fiscal 2026, 12 of which were conducted under the UNCITRAL rules.

During fiscal 2026, ICSID registered 60 new cases—57 arbitrations under the ICSID Convention and three under the Additional Facility Rules—bringing the cumulative total to 1,118 registered cases as of June 30, 2026. On the mediation front, ICSID had received a total of seven requests under Mediation Rule 6 and Mediation Rule 5, with one proceeding administered as of fiscal 2026. The year also saw 83 proceedings concluded, marking the third consecutive fiscal year in which conclusions surpassed 80.

Of the arbitrations concluded in fiscal 2026, 63 percent were decided by a tribunal and 37 percent were settled or otherwise discontinued, broadly reflecting ICSID’s long-term trend of arbitration outcomes. Among disputes decided by tribunals in the past year, 37 percent of awards upheld claims in part or in full, 40 percent rejected all claims on the merits, 18 percent declined jurisdiction, and 5 percent of cases were dismissed for manifest lack of legal merit.

In ICSID arbitrations, financial compensation is typically the primary remedy claimants seek for alleged breaches of investment obligations. Investors typically seek damages for harm they claim to have suffered as a result of measures adopted by the host State. The amounts claimed and awarded can vary significantly depending on the nature of the investment and dispute at issue.

In fiscal 2026, 70 percent of arbitration cases decided by tribunals resulted in no damages awarded to claimants, whether due to jurisdictional dismissals, findings of no liability, or liability without damages. Of the remaining cases, 14 percent resulted in awards under $10 million, 8 percent in awards between $10 million and $49 million, and 8 percent in awards exceeding $50 million.

During fiscal 2026, ICSID administered 256 sessions, hearings, and meetings. Parties and tribunals chose from in-person, virtual, or hybrid formats based on the needs of each case, with 75 percent of hearings and sessions incorporating remote features supported by ICSID's audio and video conferencing facilities. Of all the in-person hearings in fiscal 2026, 44 percent were held at ICSID’s hearing center in Washington, D.C., with the remainder taking place at venues agreed upon by the parties: Paris (30 percent), London (12 percent), Singapore (5 percent), and other locations (9 percent).

ICSID continued to see a diverse pool of arbitrators, conciliators, and committee members in fiscal 2026. The Centre recorded 250 appointments to ICSID cases, the second highest number in a single fiscal year. Appointees represented 52 different nationalities, and 29 percent of all appointments were nationals from low- or middle-income economies. First-time appointees accounted for 8 percent of all appointments; among them, 25 percent were nationals of low- or middle-income economies and 35 percent were women.

Expert Support
The ICSID Secretariat comprises 76 staff members representing 39 nationalities and speaking 22 languages, providing professional legal, administrative, and financial services across all cases. Each case is assigned a dedicated management team led by an experienced legal counsel and supported by paralegals and legal assistants. A finance and administration team oversees the financial aspects of cases and manages the planning and logistics of ICSID sessions and hearings, while an institutional matters team handles ICSID membership and the Centre’s publication program.

For more information, visit icsid.worldbank.org.
