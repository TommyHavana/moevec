The ICSID Caseload – Statistics 
(Issue 2026-2) 
This issue of the ICSID Caseload – Statistics updates the profile of the 
ICSID caseload, historically (since the registration of the first case in 1972) and for 
the fiscal year 2026 (FY2026). It is based on cases registered or administered by 
ICSID as of June 30, 2026. 
This report contains data on: 
• 
the arbitrations and conciliations registered under the ICSID Convention and 
the Additional Facility Rules (“ICSID cases”); 
• 
the mediation requests received and mediations administered by ICSID; 
• 
the non-ICSID rules cases administered by ICSID;  
• 
the basis of consent invoked for ICSID jurisdiction in ICSID cases; 
• 
the geographic distribution of ICSID cases; 
• 
the economic sectors involved in ICSID cases; 
• 
the outcomes of ICSID arbitration and conciliation proceedings, including 
information on disputes decided by arbitral tribunals, data on discontinued 
ICSID arbitration proceedings, and outcomes to date in annulment 
proceedings under the ICSID Convention; and 
• 
the nationalities, geographic origins and gender of arbitrators, conciliators 
and ad hoc committee members appointed in ICSID cases. 
The ICSID Caseload – Statistics is published bi-annually. ICSID welcomes comments 
or suggestions on the report by email at ICSIDsecretariat@worldbank.org. 
The content of this publication may be reproduced for educational use only with 
copyright acknowledgement to the International Centre for Settlement of 
Investment Disputes.

3. Mediations at ICSID 
 
In 2022, ICSID’s Member States adopted the ICSID Mediation Rules (“MR”) specifically designed 
for investor-State disputes. The ICSID Mediation Rules envision two paths for commencement of 
a mediation: the submission of a request for mediation containing a pre-existing agreement to 
mediate (MR 5) and the possibility of submitting a request for mediation that contains an offer to 
mediate, which ICSID will transmit to the other party, inviting that party to indicate within 60 
days whether it accepts or rejects such offer (MR 6). 
  
The confidentiality regime applicable to ICSID mediations is contained in MR 10 and ICSID 
Mediation Administrative and Financial Regulation 3, which provides that the Centre shall not 
publish any information related to the mediation without the agreement of the parties. Therefore, 
mediation data will be reported on in the aggregate only.  
 
As of June 30, 2026, ICSID has received one request for mediation based on a pre-existing 
agreement to mediate, and has administered that mediation. ICSID has further received 6 
requests to institute a mediation pursuant to MR 6.   
 
 
4. Non-ICSID Cases Administered by ICSID 
 
 
In addition to administering proceedings under the ICSID rules, the Centre is also available to 
administer proceedings conducted under other rules, such as the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules and 
ad hoc investor-State and State-State cases (“non-ICSID cases”).  
 
The services provided in non-ICSID cases range from limited assistance with the organization of 
hearings and management of the case finances to full secretariat services in the administration of 
the case. The ICSID Secretary-General also serves as appointing authority and decides proposals 
for disqualification of arbitrators, upon request. 
 
Chart 5 shows the number of non-ICSID cases administered by the ICSID Secretariat in FY2026. 
Chart 6 shows the distribution of UNCITRAL and other non-ICSID cases administered by the 
Centre over the past ten fiscal years. Finally, Chart 7 concerns the total number of cases 
administered by the ICSID Secretariat, under ICSID and non-ICSID rules, over the past ten 
fiscal years.

12. Disclosure of Third-Party Funding 
 
Under Rule 14 of the 2022 ICSID Convention Arbitration Rules and Rule 23 of the 2022 ICSID 
Additional Facility Arbitration Rules, parties must disclose any third-party funding received for 
the pursuit or defense of the proceeding.  
 
In FY2026, 12% of newly registered cases included such disclosures. 
 
 

 
 
 
 
The International Centre for Settlement of Investment 
Disputes (ICSID), part of the World Bank Group, is the 
world’s leading institution devoted to the resolution of 
international 
investment 
disputes. 
Its 
primary 
purpose is to promote foreign investment in its 
Member States by providing a neutral and reliable 
forum for dispute settlement between investors and 
States.  
 
ICSID administers cases under a variety of procedures, 
including arbitration, conciliation, and mediation, 
when agreed by the parties in a dispute arising from 
investment contracts, treaties, or laws. 
 
Established in 1966 under the Convention on the 
Settlement of Investment Disputes between States 
and Nationals of Other States (the ICSID Convention), 
ICSID offers a unique legal framework and specialized 
expertise that help foster favorable investment 
climates that are conducive to economic growth and 
job creation.
