Aging populations are straining retirement systems worldwide, according to the 2026 Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index

20 October, 2026
New York City United States

Marsh (NYSE: MRSH), a leading global professional services firm, and CFA Institute, the global association of investment professionals, today released the 18th annual Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index (MCGPI), comparing 55 retirement income systems across the globe, covering 66% of the world’s population. 

As fertility rates fall and life expectancies rise, the world’s retirement income systems face mounting pressure. This year’s Index examines how aging populations will impact the design and long-term sustainability of pension systems worldwide – and what policymakers should do to prepare. 

“The decisions we make today to build sustainable systems will have a profound impact on people’s financial security. Increasing longevity is too significant to ignore, with wide-ranging impacts on individuals, governments, economies and societies. The time to act is now,” said Pat Tomlinson, President and CEO of Marsh People and Investments.

“Pension systems are under real strain,” said Christine Mahoney, the Global Defined Benefit and Defined Contribution Leader of Marsh People and Investments. “Governments that clarify their pension objectives, strengthen funding, and build flexibility into retirement design will be far better positioned to deliver dignified, secure retirements for the generations ahead.”

Mona Naqvi, Managing Director of the CFA Institute Research and Policy Center, added: “Retirement systems differ across markets, but all face the same long-term challenge: delivering reliable income as demographics shift and markets change. No single policy or investment decision can achieve that. It takes sound system design and governance, alongside careful oversight of investments by those with a fiduciary duty to savers and retirees. The test is whether those decisions work together to serve people throughout retirement and remain sound for the generations that follow.”

Adapting systems to a longer, more flexible retirement

The 2026 Index highlights three key policy responses needed to address these demographic realities: establishing clear objectives for pension systems; prioritizing the funding of future retirement obligations; and increasing flexibility in pension product design and retirement age structures. Traditional retirement ages of 60 or 65 are becoming increasingly misaligned with the realities of longer, healthier lives. As the boundary between work and retirement continues to blur – driven in part by the rise of gig and part-time work – pension systems must evolve to reflect the way people live and work in later life.

“The data shows that even the best-performing systems need to keep evolving; no system can afford to stand still in the face of these demographic shifts,” commented Tim Jenkins, lead author of the report and Partner at Marsh People and Investments. “The right approach for one country, one system, or even one individual will not be the right approach for another. But what every system shares is the need to adapt. Retirement design must become more flexible – in when people access income, in how products are structured, and in how we think about the line between work and retirement. That flexibility is no longer optional.”

While disparities persist, meaningful improvements are apparent across the Index

The retirement income systems of the Netherlands, Denmark, Iceland, Singapore and Israel retained their A grade in 2026 with Index scores greater than 80. For the first time, Australia has also received an A grade. These six systems are robust: they deliver good benefits, are sustainable and have a high level of integrity. 

The Index uses the weighted average of the sub-indices of adequacy, sustainability and integrity. For each sub-index, the systems with the highest values were Kuwait for adequacy, Denmark for sustainability and Finland for integrity.

This year, five retirement income systems – Australia, UAE, Oman, Panama and China – have improved their Index grade. The average value for the overall Index has increased by 1.1 points from 2025.

About CFA Institute

As the global association of investment professionals, CFA Institute sets the standards for professional excellence and credentials. We champion ethical behavior in investment markets and serve as the leading source of learning and research for the investment industry. We believe in fostering an environment where investors’ interests come first, markets function at their best, and economies grow. With more than 200,000 charterholders worldwide across more than 160 markets, CFA Institute has 8 offices and 157 local societies. Find us at www.cfainstitute.org or follow us on LinkedIn and subscribe on YouTube.

About the Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index (MCGPI)

The MCGPI benchmarks retirement income systems around the world, highlights strengths and suggests possible areas of reform that would provide more adequate and sustainable retirement benefits with a high level of integrity. This year, it compares 55 retirement income systems across the globe, including three new countries – the Dominican Republic, Kenya and Qatar – and covers 66% of the world’s population.

The Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index is a collaborative research project co-sponsored by CFA Institute and Marsh and is supported by the Monash Centre for Financial Studies (MCFS). Find more information about the Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index here.

About the Monash Centre for Financial Studies (MCFS)

A research centre based within Monash University's Monash Business School, Australia, the MCFS aims to bring academic rigour into researching issues of practical relevance to the financial industry. Additionally, through its engagement programs, it facilitates two-way exchange of knowledge between academics and practitioners. The Centre’s developing research agenda is broad but has a current concentration on issues relevant to the asset management industry, including retirement savings, sustainable finance and technological disruption.
