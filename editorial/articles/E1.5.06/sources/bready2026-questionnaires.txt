Description of B-READY Questionnaires 
 
Business Entry questionnaire 
The Business Entry questionnaire measures three dimensions: the Quality of Regulations for Business Entry, 
the Digital Public Services and Transparency of Information for Business Entry, and the Operational 
Efficiency of Business Entry. The first dimension assesses the quality of regulations for business entry, 
covering de jure features of a regulatory framework to measure the adoption of good practices for business 
startups on information and procedural standards, and restrictions on registering a business. for domestic 
and foreign firms. The second measures the availability of digital public services, the interoperability of 
services and transparency of online information for business startups. The third pillar measures the time 
and cost required to register a new domestic and foreign firm. Finally, the third dimension (Operational 
Efficiency of Business Entry) measures the total time and cost to register new domestic and foreign firms. 
Business Location- Property Transfer questionnaire 
The Property Transfer questionnaire measures three dimensions: quality of regulations for property transfer, 
digital public services and transparency of information for property transfer, and operational efficiency of 
property transfer. The first dimension assesses the quality of regulations for property transfer, covering de 
jure features of property-related regulatory framework, such as property transfer standards, land dispute 
mechanisms, land administration system, property market governance and restrictions on owning and 
leasing property. The second measures availability and reliability of digital public services for property 
transfer, coverage and features of digital land management, interoperability of services and transparency of 
information for property transfer. The third pillar measures firm perceptions of access to land as a constraint, 
as well as the time and cost required to transfer a property. 
Business Location- Construction  Permits questionnaire 
The Construction Permits questionnaire measures three dimensions: quality of regulations for building and 
environmental permits, digital public services and transparency of information for construction permitting, 
and operational efficiency of obtaining building and environmental permits. The first dimension assesses 
the quality of regulations for construction, covering building standards (including structural safety, fire 
safety, health and sanitation requirements, and inspection regimes), building energy efficiency standards, 
zoning and land use regulations, environmental permit requirements for construction, and dispute resolution 
mechanisms. The second dimension measures the availability and reliability of digital public services for 
building and environmental permits, interoperability of services (such as access to spatial plans and zoning 
requirements through GIS platforms), and transparency of information including publicly available building 
regulations, permit requirements, fee schedules, and environmental licensing requirements. The third 
dimension measures the time and cost required to obtain building permits and environmental permits, using 
two standardized commercial building scenarios to ensure comparability across economies. 
 
Utility Services- Electricity questionnaire 
The Electricity questionnaire measures three dimensions: the quality of regulations, the quality of 
governance and the transparency of electricity service provision and the operational efficiency of electricity 
service provision. The quality of regulations is assessed by evaluating the proper monitoring of tariffs and 
adherence to service quality standards, the normative framework to facilitate infrastructure sharing, the

sustainable practices in electricity provision and electricity use, as well as renewable energy integration. 
The second dimension on governance of the governance and transparency of electricity services in turn

Labor questionnaire 
The Labor questionnaire evaluates two key dimensions: the quality of labor regulations and the adequacy 
of public services for labor. The quality of labor regulations is assessed by examining labor regulations 
related to workers' conditions, employment restrictions, and associated costs. This includes questions on 
labor rights, minimum wage, terms of employment, termination procedures, and regulations related to 
telework, platform work, and informality. The adequacy of public services for labor focuses on social 
protection and employment services. Accordingly, the questions explore the availability and funding of 
unemployment insurance, healthcare, and retirement pensions, as well as employment services that support 
labor market functioning, such as employment centers, labor dispute resolution mechanisms, and labor 
inspectorates. It also considers the existence of basic labor statistics.

Financial Services- Commercial Lending (Customer Due Diligence) questionnaire 
This questionnaire assesses the regulatory framework governing Customer Due Diligence (CDD) 
requirements for commercial lenders throughout the commercial loan application process. It examines 
whether lenders must verify customer identities, determine beneficial ownership, and apply a risk-based 
approach when assessing potential money laundering or terrorism financing risks. The questionnaire also 
evaluates the availability and use of enhanced and simplified CDD measures, as well as guidance provided 
by supervisory authorities to support effective implementation. In addition, it reviews regulations related to 
digital identity systems, e-KYC utilities, third-party KYC providers, and associated data protection and 
record-keeping obligations. Finally, it assesses the regulatory environment for digital commercial lending, 
including open banking, regulatory sandboxes, and innovation hubs that support the testing and 
development of new digital lending models.  
Financial Services- Electronic Payments (E-Payments) questionnaire 
This questionnaire assesses the regulatory framework governing e-payments and the requirements that 
apply to payment service providers (PSPs). It evaluates risk management obligations, including internal 
controls, external reviews, cybersecurity, operational resilience, liquidity safeguards, and business 
continuity planning. The questionnaire also examines consumer protection measures such as strong 
customer authentication, rules for canceling or modifying transactions, liability for unauthorized payments, 
safeguarding of customer funds, disclosure obligations, data protection, and dispute-resolution mechanisms. 
In addition, it reviews transparency requirements, including pricing disclosures, customer notifications, and 
clear guidance on service terms and conditions. The interoperability section evaluates whether payment 
instruments and systems function seamlessly across providers and whether regulations promote open access, 
fair competition, and non-discriminatory treatment of PSPs. It further assesses supervisory oversight of 
payment systems and market conduct to ensure reliability and fairness. The questionnaire also considers 
the regulatory environment for innovation, including the availability of regulatory sandboxes and 
innovation hubs. Additionally, it includes a section on cross-border electronic payments for the 
International Trade topic. 
Financial Services- Green and Sustainable Finance questionnaire (non-scored questionnaire – only 
for a selected sample of economies)  
The questionnaire evaluates how effectively an economy’s financial system integrates environmental and 
climate considerations, measuring its regulatory, institutional, and market readiness for sustainable finance. 
It examines taxonomies, disclosure rules, risk management practices, and national strategies, as well as 
frameworks for green bonds, loans, sustainability-linked instruments, and blended finance. It also considers 
enforcement mechanisms, prevention of greenwashing, capacity building, data availability, and technical 
support, alongside market barriers and regional cooperation. The resulting data maps access to sustainable 
finance by showing which projects qualify, what standards firms must meet, and what incentives and 
instruments are available.  
 
Financial Services – Secured Transactions and Operation of Collateral Registries questionnaire 
The Secured Transactions and Operation of Collateral Registries questionnaire assesses the legal and 
regulatory framework for Secured Transactions by examining whether legal provisions on security interests 
over movable assets and de facto features of collateral registries facilitate lending and protect the rights of

borrowers and lenders. Secured transactions refer to all transactions, irrespective of the form, that create a 
right in any type of movable asset to secure the performance of an obligation. The secured transactions 
section measures different key aspects such as the integration of functional equivalents into the secured 
transactions framework, the types of movable assets, debts and obligations that can be secured and the 
priority and out-of-court enforcement of the secured rights focusing on private sale and appropriation. The 
collateral registries section examines their existence and set up; coverage of transactions, debtors and 
creditors, as well as geographical coverage; operation and modern features; and reliability of data in 
collateral registries.  
Financial Services – Operation of Credit bureaus and Registries questionnaire 
The Operation of Credit Bureaus and Registries questionnaire measures the rules and practices affecting 
the coverage, scope, and accessibility of credit information available in economies through Credit Reporting 
Services Providers (either private bureaus and/or public registries) (“CRSPs”) used to facilitate lending 
decisions. The questionnaire is structured to gather information in several key areas. Firstly, it assesses 
fundamental principles governing CRSPs, including applicable data privacy and protection rules, and 
CRSPs’ risk management. Secondly, it assesses data management (retention and removal), data quality and 
timeliness, data security, and data coverage (focusing on inclusion of traditional and alternative data 
sources). Thirdly, the questionnaire explores CRSPs’ advanced features, such as cross border data sharing 
and crisis-related data, data subject and consumer rights, and additional services offered such as credit 
scores, credit monitoring, and fraud detection and tracking. 
International Trade- Trade Regulation–Goods questionnaire 
The Trade Regulations - Goods questionnaire measures the domestic legal framework governing trade in 
goods. It is organized into five sections. The first section examines the legal framework governing customs, 
including temporary admission, de minimis regimes, advance rulings, and mechanisms for appealing or 
reviewing customs decisions. The second section evaluates the regulatory governance for adopting tariff 
and non-tariff measures. The third section focuses on sustainable trade policy, with questions on permits to 
trade in endangered species and on the regulation of exports of hazardous waste and persistent organic 
pollutants. The fourth section evaluates the transparency, extent, and depth of trade cooperation. Most 
questions assess whether justiciable commitments exist with the three main trading partners in specific 
areas such as mutual recognition of conformity assessment or of professional qualifications and 
certifications. The final section assesses the existence of selected technical and non-technical measures. 
 
International Trade- Trade Regulation–Services questionnaire 
 The Trade Regulations Services questionnaire measures the domestic legal framework governing trade in 
services in five sectors: maritime freight transport, air freight transport, road freight transport, customs 
brokerage, and logistics. Questions on freight transportation are asked only if the economy has actual trade 
flows in the relevant mode. For example, landlocked economies are not asked questions on maritime freight 
transport. The questionnaire is organized into five sections, each corresponding to one of the sectors. Every 
section assesses market access conditions for foreign and domestic firms. In addition, each section includes 
questions on other good regulatory practices and on sustainability, where relevant.

International Trade- Digital Trade questionnaire 
The Digital Trade questionnaire brings together questions addressed to telecommunications, media and 
technology (TMT) experts, financial services experts, and tax experts. TMT experts are asked about 
regulations governing cross border data, as well as rules on electronic commerce and consumer protection. 
Financial services experts are asked about regulations that govern cross border electronic payments. Finally, 
tax experts are asked about the treatment of value added tax or goods and services tax (VAT/GST) for non-
residents. 
 
International Trade- Trade Regulation–Public Services questionnaire 
The Public Services questionnaire measures the availability, extent, and functioning of public services that 
facilitate trade. It comprises nine sections. The first section assesses the availability and quality of online 
information on import and export procedures, including information on tariff and non-tariff measures. The 
second section examines the availability of electronic services for customs clearance, including the 
processes to obtain SPS, standards, technical, and environmental certificates. The third section focuses on 
the functioning of risk assessment in customs procedures. The fourth section evaluates the availability and 
functioning of National Trade Facilitation Committees. The fifth section reviews the availability of trusted 
trader programs for a wide range of supply chain operators, as well as the benefits these programs provide. 
Sections six, seven, and eight assess the availability of selected trade infrastructure facilities and services 
at the main customs border posts (seaport, airport, and land border). These sections are applicable only to 
economies that have at least one functioning border post in the relevant category. For example, the seaport 
section is not available for landlocked economies. Section 9 measures the availability of customs border 
management services, both domestically through inter agency coordination and internationally through 
cooperation with selected trading partners. 
 
Taxation questionnaire 
The Taxation questionnaire measures three dimensions of a tax system: the quality of regulations, public 
services provided by the tax administration, and their implementation. The quality of tax regulations is 
assessed by examining the clarity, transparency, and administrative procedures, incorporating de jure and 
de facto information on the legal requirements. It also captures regulations pertaining to the environmental 
fiscal instruments implemented in the economy.  
The quality of public services provided by the tax administration is evaluated through the digital services 
for taxpayers, data management and systems integration, transparency of the tax administration, as well as 
tax audits and related disputes. Some of the questions also reflect the measures of operational efficiency of 
tax processes from a firm’s perspective.

The Dispute Resolution questionnaire measures efficiency and quality of the resolution of commercial 
disputes—those arising in the business context between firms—across three different dimensions (pillars). 
The first pillar assesses the adequacy of legislation pertaining to both court processes and alternative dispute 
resolution, covering de jure features that are necessary for the efficient processing of cases, facilitated 
resolution of cross-border claims, creating alternative venues for settling disputes, and ensuring trust in 
relevant institutions. The second pillar focuses on judicial organizational structure, courts’ digitalization 
and transparency, as well as ADR-related services, thus capturing the de facto provision of public services. 
The third pillar measures the reliability of dispute resolution, the time and cost required to resolve a dispute, 
as well as the time and cost associated with the recognition of foreign arbitral awards and compulsory 
enforcement of judgments. 
 
 
Market Competition – Competition law questionnaire 
The competition law questionnaire targets key regulations and public services that promote competitive 
behaviors and effective implementation of competition regulations. The questionnaire begins by identifying 
the main competition regulatory instruments and the basic institutional framework of the Competition 
Authority in an economy. It then addresses regulations that could limit the scope of Competition Law, 
focusing on the notion of competitive neutrality as applied to State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) and the 
process for excluding firms or sectors from the application of Competition Law. The next section examines 
regulations that identify and forbid anticompetitive behaviors and the enforcement of competition 
regulations. This includes matters related to antitrust, abuse of dominance, leniency, the Competition 
Authority’s capacity to investigate and impose fines, and the procedural guarantees and legal resources 
available to firms. Further, the questionnaire highlights good practices related to merger control, which 
aims to prevent anticompetitive market concentration resulting from mergers or acquisitions. It examines 
whether such transactions are likely to adversely affect competition in the market for goods or services, 
focusing solely on competition-related aspects. Finally, the questionnaire explores the institutional 
framework of Competition Authorities and their role in advocating for competition policy to firms in the 
market. 
 
Market Competition – Procurement questionnaire 
The Public Procurement questionnaire measures key aspects of the public procurement cycle to ensure that 
contracts are awarded in a manner that promotes open, competitive, and transparent processes. Using the 
procuring entity as a parameter to enable comparability in responses, the first section is designed to assess 
the regulatory framework supporting participation, fairness, and transparency in bidding for public 
contracts, covering good practices in tender design and contract implementation. The assessment measures 
the applicability of procurement regulations to all entities including SOEs and independent authorities. It 
further evaluates the presence of clearly defined rules and guidelines for standardization in procurement 
plans, and the use of model tender documents with gender and sustainability components. Additionally, it 
examines procedural aspects between bid advertisement and bid submission, focusing on fairness, equal 
opportunities for firms, and reduced barriers to entry for SMEs and foreign firms. This section also evaluates

the existence of provisions aimed at improving best value for money, promoting environmental 
sustainability in bids, and fostering innovation. The second section in the questionnaire is dedicated to 
assessing the degree of digitalization and sophistication of the procurement system, particularly through the 
existence of a centralized procurement platform and its usage by all procuring entities. Finally, the last 
section measures the efficiency of governments in implementing regulations and public services. Key 
indicators covered include the timeliness of contract awards and government payments, as well as firms’ 
perceptions of bidding ease and the gender gap in government suppliers. 
 
Market Competition – Innovation questionnaire 
The Innovation questionnaire targets key regulations and practices that promote innovation and technology 
transfer through three dimensions. Under the first dimension (regulatory framework) the Innovation 
indicators measure quality of regulations, which include strength of intellectual property rights protection, 
licensing and technology transfer, open access and fair use of innovation – including environment and 
university-industry collaboration. The second dimension (public services) covers public services enabling 
innovation in firms. This includes indicators on digitalization of intellectual property services, innovation 
systems -including gender- and institutional framework to support innovation. The third and final 
dimension (efficiency) focuses on efficiency of innovation and intellectual property rights regulations, 
identifying highly innovative firms, and the use of international quality certifications. 
 
Business Insolvency questionnaire 
 The Business Insolvency topic measures key features of insolvency systems on a regulatory level. It also 
assesses the institutional and operational infrastructure associated with insolvency proceedings (judicial 
services), as well as the operational efficiency of insolvency proceedings across three different dimensions, 
referred to as pillars. The first pillar assesses the quality of regulation pertaining to judicial insolvency 
proceedings—liquidation and reorganization, covering de jure features of a regulatory framework that are 
necessary for structured debt resolution processes and effective creditor and debtor regimes. The second 
pillar measures the quality of institutional and operational infrastructure for judicial insolvency proceedings, 
thus assessing the de facto aspects of insolvency resolution mechanisms and the infrastructure required to 
implement the legal framework on insolvency. The third pillar measures the time and cost required to 
resolve in-court liquidation and reorganization proceedings.
