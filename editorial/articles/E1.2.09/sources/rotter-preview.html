Generalized expectancies for internal versus external control of reinforcement examine how perceived contingency shapes behavior. The report synthesizes experiments comparing group differences when subjects view reinforcement as contingent on their own actions versus determined by chance or experimenter control. It also describes development of tests for individual differences in internal-external control beliefs, reporting reliability, discriminant validity, normative data for one test, and results supporting construct validity across multiple studies.

FAQs

What determines whether reinforcement is perceived as internal or external control?

Perception depends on whether the reward is viewed as contingent on one’s own behavior or attributes (internal control) versus resulting from luck, chance, fate, or powerful others (external control).

What does the report aim to summarize?

It summarizes experiments that define group differences in behavior based on whether reinforcement is perceived as contingent on the subject or controlled by chance/experimenter factors.

What psychometric information is provided for the internal-external control tests?

The report describes reliability, discriminant validity, and normative data for one test, and it discusses results related to construct validity from multiple studies.
