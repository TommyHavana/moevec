EU businesses: €10.9 trillion in value added in 2024
© Stockbym/stock.adobe.com
In 2024, there were around 34.0 million
enterprises
in the
EU
, employing 164.5 million people. Those enterprises generated a
net turnover
of over €38.7 trillion, leading to €10.9 trillion in
value added
.
Large enterprises (with more than 249 people employed) represented only 0.2% of the total number of enterprises in the EU’s business economy. However, they employed more than a third of the business economy workforce (37%) and generated around half (49%) of the total value added.
Medium-sized enterprises (50-249 people employed) made up a small share of total enterprises (0.8%), employed 15% of the workforce, and generated 16% of value added.
The majority, 99%, of the enterprises were micro and small enterprises (0-49 people employed). Together, these enterprises employed almost half of all people employed in the EU’s business economy (48%) and generated 35% of its total value added.
This information comes from final data on
structural business statistics
(SBS) for 2024, published by Eurostat today.
Source dataset:
sbs_sc_ovw
Services: highest share in employment and in value added
The services sector generated half (51%) of the total value added of the EU’s business economy in 2024, the highest share among the 4 main sectors (industry, trade, services and construction). With 64% of all enterprises, it employed more than half of the workforce (53%).
The industry sector accounted for 28% of value added, with only 7% of total  enterprises, and around one-fifth of total employment (20%).
Trade represented 15% of value added but accounted for 17% of the enterprises and employed 18% of the people.
Construction enterprises represented 12% of the total number of enterprises but produced only 7% of the total value added. This sector accounted for 8% of employment in the EU’s business economy.
Source dataset:
sbs_sc_ovw

Methodological notes
The term ‘business economy’ is defined as ‘industry’ (definition below), ‘construction’, ‘distributive trades’ (wholesale and retail trades) and ‘services’ (definition see below), and covers only ‘
market producers
’. It therefore excludes public administration and defence, compulsory social security, and the activities of membership organisations.
Micro, small, medium-sized and large enterprises in structural business statistics (SBS) are defined solely by the number of people employed. They do not take into account other factors such as turnover and balance sheet totals. Enterprises can belong to larger enterprise groups.
The number of ‘people employed’ includes employees and self-employed people.
In SBS, the term ‘industry’ covers the following sections of the
NACE
: business enterprises in ‘mining and quarrying’; ‘manufacturing’; ‘electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning supply’; and ‘water supply; sewerage, waste management and remediation activities’.
In SBS, the term ‘services’ covers the following segments of the NACE: business enterprises in sections ‘transportation and storage’; ‘accommodation and food service activities’; ‘information and communication’; ‘financial and insurance activities’; ‘real estate activities’; ‘professional, scientific and technical activities’; ‘administrative and support service activities’; ‘education’; ‘human health and social work activities’; ‘arts, entertainment and recreation’; and divisions ‘repair of computers and personal and household goods’; ‘other personal service activities’.
