1. Executive Summary
Despite the complexities of today’s media 
landscape, fact-checking organizations 
around the world are actively seeking IFCN 
verification — demonstrating a clear demand 
for global credibility and professional 
standards. In 2024 alone, the IFCN handled 
more than 200 requests for certification 
from organizations across 57 countries and 
six continents. This represents a significant 
validation of the Code’s continued relevance 
and the global community’s desire to be seen 
as legitimate, trusted arbiters of information.
This comes at a time when fact-checkers 
face coordinated harassment, legal threats 
and shrinking financial support — a reality 
highlighted in recent research from the Global 
Disinformation Index (2024) and the Reuters 
Institute (2023), which both cite growing 
hostility toward fact-checkers as part of 
broader attacks on journalism. 
Despite this, organizations are not 
withdrawing from public accountability; they 
are leaning into it. The International Fact-
Checking Network continues to strengthen 
and expand its global certification framework; 
this report highlights the IFCN’s activities, 
achievements, and strategic improvements 
implemented throughout 2024.
Growth and Global Reach
In 2024, the IFCN handled 226 applications 
(165 renewals and 61 new applications) and 
completed assessment of 135 applications, 
with 116 organizations successfully meeting 
compliance standards. These verified 
signatories now represent 56 countries across 
six continents, demonstrating the expanding 
influence of professional fact-checking 
In an era marked by increasing threats to press freedom, politicized 
disinformation and rising hostility toward journalists, the International 
Fact-Checking Network (IFCN) has reinforced its commitment to 
strengthening global accountability and trust in fact-checking. The IFCN 
Code of Principles continues to serve as the world’s leading standard for 
transparent, nonpartisan fact-checking organizations.
globally. At the time this report was finalized, 
the IFCN maintained 182 verified signatories 
committed to upholding the Code’s high 
standards.
Operational Improvements
Significant process improvements 
implemented in 2024 have streamlined 
operations and reduced average assessment 
time from eight to 10 months to approximately 
six months. Key improvements included:
• Introduction of a preliminary screening 
process for new applicants.
• Implementation of a two-year review 
cycle for established signatories with five 
successful reviews.
• Enhanced assessor recruitment and 
training.
• Comprehensive website overhaul for 
improved user experience and application 
tracking.
• Revised complaints process to reduce 
frivolous submissions and better identify 
legitimate concerns.
Governance and 
Community Support
The advisory board was expanded to 
include eight new members from diverse 
regions, bringing broader global perspectives. 
Working groups were established to 
address specific challenges such as financial 
sustainability and harassment response. The 
IFCN also implemented dedicated webinars 
and consultations for Code of Principles 
applicants, improving communication and 
support.
Strategic Initiatives for 2025
Looking ahead, the IFCN will focus on:
• Comprehensive research for a revision of 
the Code of Principles to address emerging 
challenges, including AI implications and 
coalition fact-checking programs.
• Development of a self-paced online 
training course on applying for the Code of 
Principles signatory status.
• Further web platform enhancements to 
improve usability and automation.
• Continued process improvements through 
standardized assessor guidelines and 
targeted automation.
Through these initiatives, the IFCN aims 
to maintain the Code’s position as the 
international standard for fact-checking 
journalism while adapting to new challenges 
in the evolving information ecosystem.

The Code and its Significance
Trust forms the cornerstone of effective 
fact-checking journalism. According to the 
Reuters Institute’s comprehensive Digital 
News Report 2024, which surveyed 47 media 
markets across six continents, transparency 
and adherence to high journalistic standards 
emerged as the two most critical factors 
driving public trust in news sources. In this 
landscape, fact-checkers serve a vital role by 
alerting readers to dangerous misinformation 
through methodical verification. The Code 
establishes a framework comprising five 
fundamental principles, 
supported by 31 specific 
criteria that organizations 
must satisfy to 
demonstrate compliance. 
Developed collaboratively 
by practicing fact-
checkers, these principles 
create a foundation for 
methodological transparency, enhance public 
trust, and foster evidence-based discourse in 
the global information ecosystem.
The first principle is a commitment 
to nonpartisanship and fairness. This 
means that fact-checking organizations and 
teams must follow a standardized process of 
evidence for every fact check, regardless of 
the fact-checking journalist’s stance on policy 
positions or ideologies. This is the foundation 
of the Code and is an essential building block 
of high-quality fact-checking. Fact checks 
must be balanced and evidence-driven, and 
the organization’s operations and policies 
must support the same standard. For example, 
to be compliant, a fact-checking organization, 
or its parent organization when applicable, 
cannot be affiliated with or show support for 
any political party, any politician or political 
candidate, nor advocate for or against any 
policy positions save for transparency and 
accuracy in public debate. Furthermore, 
it must have a publicly accessible 
nonpartisanship policy on its website. Staff 
members must maintain neutrality on policy 
2. What is the 
Code of Principles?
Fact-checkers from around the world founded the IFCN in late 2015 to 
uphold accuracy, transparency, and accountability within the global fact-
checking community. Amid the rising tide of misinformation, it became 
evident that a unified set of standards was essential. In response, IFCN 
launched the Code in 2016, establishing guidelines for fact-checkers 
worldwide. The Code of Principles today serves as the foundational 
framework for the network, establishing the standards and practices that 
define its mission. 
Within the first year, 35 organizations from 
27 countries joined the IFCN as signatories to 
the Code. Today, the number of fact-checking 
organizations that adhere to the Code of 
Principles has grown significantly – 182 
verified signatories representing 81 countries 
and a broad spectrum of organizations 
committed to high standards in fact-checking. 
The signatories range from major news outlets 
and international agencies to small nonprofits 
and innovative startup projects. These 
organizations operate in diverse political and 
media environments, but share a common 
commitment to the Code’s high standards. 
By guiding high-quality fact-checking across 
contexts — from multiparty democracies 
to regions with limited access to reliable 
information — the Code builds credibility 
and public trust in fact-checking journalism 
worldwide.

in 2016 began using it as a guiding standard

They cannot engage in advocacy or express

public to perceive bias in the organization’s

safeguards reinforcing the first principle’s 
commitment to nonpartisanship and fairness.
The second principle establishes 
a commitment to standards and 
transparency of sources. This cornerstone 
defines rigorous protocols for verifying 
claims, evaluating evidence hierarchies, 
disclosing information sources, and ensuring 
fact checks remain fully replicable by readers. 
By implementing systematic methodological 
frameworks, fact-checking organizations 
minimize source bias while maintaining 
transparent documentation practices 
throughout their verification process.
When addressing economic or policy 
matters, for example, organizations 
typically triangulate evidence from multiple 
perspectives — combining government 
datasets, expert analyses and community 
impact assessments to present comprehensive 
verification. Fact checks should provide 
detailed source attribution that includes each 
source’s identification, specific relevance to 
the claim under examination, and established 
expertise in the relevant field. Organizations 
must also disclose contextual factors that 
might influence evidence interpretation.
In cases where primary evidence remains 
inaccessible to the public, organizations must 
publish clear explanations for their reliance 
on secondary sources. The fundamental 
responsibility of fact-checkers under this 
principle is to construct evidence chains 
that are logical, accessible and traceable — 
allowing readers to independently follow 
the verification pathway that supports each 
published conclusion.
The third principle is a commitment to 
transparency of funding and organization. 
This section outlines how fact-checking 
organizations and fact-checking teams 
within larger organizations are to disclose 
their funding sources or ownership and 
maintain editorial control and independence. 
These financial and ownership transparency 
requirements help readers identify potential 
influences or conflicts of interest, understand 
how the organization is funded, who the 
funders are and who makes editorial 
decisions.
The fourth principle is a commitment 
to standards and transparency of 
methodology. Fact-checking processes 
are directly informed by an organization’s 
methodological approach to verifying 
information and analyzing claims. 
Organizations must provide a detailed 
methodology page that defines what can and 
cannot be fact-checked, how sources are 
verified and how evidence is evaluated. It 
should explain how evidence is handled when 
it supports or challenges the central claim, 
how primary and secondary sources are 
distinguished, how the organization handles 
requests for comment from claimants and how 
readers can submit claims for review.
The IFCN verifies that the methodology is 
consistently applied across fact checks and 
that the published fact checks remain neutral 
and follow consistent evidence standards to 
ensure nonpartisanship.
The fifth principle establishes a 
commitment to an open and honest 
corrections policy. This cornerstone not 
only ensures transparency when errors 
occur but embodies the intellectual integrity 
essential to credible fact-checking. Self-
correction — the willingness to acknowledge 
and rectify mistakes rather than defending 
inaccuracies — represents the highest form of 
journalistic accountability and demonstrates 
an organization’s authentic commitment to 
truth over self-interest.
Errors may arise from various sources: 
copy-editing oversights, sourcing limitations 
or the emergence of new evidence that 
contradicts initial conclusions. To uphold 
this principle, organizations must implement 
a comprehensive corrections framework 
that explicitly defines both minor and 
substantive errors, ensures corrected content 
is prominently labeled and maintains 
accessibility for readers seeking accurate 
information. Additionally, organizations 
must provide straightforward mechanisms 
for audiences to submit correction requests 
or lodge complaints regarding published fact 
checks.
For established signatories, maintaining a 
visible link to the IFCN complaints policy 
creates an additional layer of accountability. 
Organizations must clearly communicate 
to readers that while the IFCN doesn’t 
adjudicate disputes about individual fact 
checks, it does oversee systematic compliance 
with the Code’s standards. This multi-
tiered accountability structure — internal 
corrections processes, reader feedback 
channels, and IFCN oversight — creates a 
self-regulating ecosystem that continually 
reinforces fact-checking integrity while 
building public trust in the verification 
process.
Organizations are not 
withdrawing from 
public accountability; 
they are leaning into 
it. The International 
Fact-Checking Network 
continues to strengthen 
and expand its global 
certification framework.

Eligibility
To become a signatory to the IFCN Code 
of Principles, fact-checking organizations 
must submit an eligibility request to the 
IFCN before applying. The eligibility 
criteria include:
1. A link to the applicant’s website and 
their legal registration page.
2. The type of fact-checking organization: 
nonprofit, for-profit, or part of a larger 
media group.
3. Years of operation and evidence of 
published fact checks.
4. A link to their detailed nonpartisanship 
policy.
5. A link to their detailed methodology 
page.
6. A link to their detailed complaints 
page.
Applications are reviewed by assessors, 
independent professionals with expertise 
in fact-checking, the local media 
environment and language proficiency 
from the applicant’s region. They evaluate 
the strength of the applicant’s fact-
checking methodology and confirm that 
transparency requirements are met, as 
outlined in the Code. Whether a new or 
renewing applicant, the organization 
must meet the following criteria during 
the assessment:
1. Be a legally registered organization, 
unit or team within a legally registered 
entity.
2. The organization or team must 
exclusively work on fact-checking.
3. It must publicly display its fact-
checking methodology, define the

open and transparent corrections policy.
4. It should publish an average of at least one 
fact check per week over six months, or 12 
months for fact-checking organizations or 
teams in countries with five or more active 
IFCN signatories.
5. At least 75% of fact checks must focus on 
claims that impact the welfare or well-
being of individuals, the general public or 
society.
6. Funding sources that account for 5% 
or more of total annual revenue must be 
disclosed, or a statement of ownership

7. Editorial output must not be controlled 
or influenced by a state, political party or

8. The organization must document how it 
ensures editorial control and independence 
from funders.
Waiver Requests
While the standards outlined above are 
required, new and renewing applicants may 
request a waiver from one or more of these 
criteria when compliance is not possible, or 
when disclosure could compromise the safety 
and security of the fact-checkers. 
For example, applicants may request waivers 
from financial disclosures or organizational 
information if such information would cause 
material harm to the organization or team 
members because of their journalism. 
Given the rise in harassment against 
journalists and threats to press freedoms, 
applicants may also request a waiver from 
the weekly publication requirements if 
the organization is operating in regions 
experiencing civil or political conflict or in 
regions hostile to independent journalism.
If a waiver is requested, the applicant must 
submit it with their application for review 
by the assessor and the advisory board. The 
applicant must provide information regarding 
their media environment, and they must 
explain any safety risks or unique challenges 
that could arise if the information were made 
public. This typically includes supporting 
financial and organizational documents. 
These documents are filed separately from 
the application and only viewable by IFCN 
staff, the assessor and the advisory board. 
The assessor reviews them to determine their 
merit and to provide a recommendation to the 
IFCN advisory board on whether the waiver 
should be accepted or rejected. The IFCN 
advisory board has discretion in accepting or 
rejecting an applicant’s waiver request.
Due to significant changes to the global 
media environment, particularly concerning 
foreign funding and “foreign agent” laws, the 
IFCN received 18 waiver requests in 2024. 
Of the waivers submitted, nine were from 
renewing signatories and the other nine came 
from new applicants. 
Most applicants requested waivers from 
financial disclosure requirements, and these 
applicants typically operated in regions where 
press freedoms had eroded, where journalists 
faced harassment or where they were targets 
of repressive “foreign agent” laws. 
Two applicants requested leniency on 
weekly fact check requirements. These 
organizations experienced severe and 
persistent harassment and legal threats from 
political entities, forcing them to pause their 
operations. Others cited financial constraints 
that limited their publishing capacity.
Upon review of these waivers in the 
assessment process, only one new applicant 
was rejected due to shortcomings in 
their sourcing policy and fact-checking 
methodology. The remaining 17 waivers were 
reviewed and approved by the IFCN advisory 
board.
Assessment Process
The IFCN’s assessment process balances 
thoroughness with efficiency through a 
structured evaluation. Central to this system 
are independent assessors who operate 
separately from both IFCN staff and the 
advisory board, bringing crucial regional 
expertise and language proficiency to each 
application. Their deep understanding of local 
media environments ensures applications are 
evaluated within appropriate cultural and 
political contexts while maintaining global 
standards. This independence strengthens the 
integrity of the entire certification system.
Timelines for the assessment process 
vary depending on the application volume 
and whether the applicant is a new or 
renewing signatory. Due to the number of 
applications, the availability of assessors and 
the length of advisory board deliberation, the 
application process for new organizations 
may take between six months to 18 months 
from submission to final decision. Renewals 
typically take about six months from 
submission to final review.1 Assessments may 
take longer if the advisory board wishes to 
extend their voting period to request more 
information from the applicant or assessor. 
All assessments, whether new or renewing, 
follow the same five stages: 
• Assessor confirmation: Assessors are 
vetted journalism and fact-checking 
professionals from around the globe, 
typically college professors, lecturers or 
media experts. IFCN staff review each 
new assessor’s qualifications. After they 
are deemed eligible and attend a webinar, 
they may start work as an assessor. They 
are assigned to specific applications 
based on availability, regional expertise, 
country, language needs, and familiarity 
with the applicant’s media and political 
environment. 
• Assessment: The IFCN currently partners 
with 51 assessors to review applications. 
Assessors typically have 15 days to 
complete preliminary assessments and 
flag non-compliant submissions to the

detailed feedback and may reapply later,

application is sent to the IFCN advisory 
board, which is the body that approves 
or rejects applicants. The advisory board 
members — 15 when all seats are filled — 
deliberate on each completed application, 
reviewing assessor notes and supporting 
documents to ensure the applicant meets 
the Code’s standards. The advisory board 
has four to six weeks to complete its review 
of the applications and vote in favor or 
against the acceptance of the applicant as a 
signatory. The advisory board may extend 
its voting period on specific applications 
for further deliberation and review, 
requesting additional information from the 
applicant or assessor to make an informed 
decision. If the advisory board reviews 
the application and finds it compliant with 
the Code, the applicant is accepted as a 
verified signatory, eligible for professional 
development opportunities managed by the 
IFCN. 
New Applications
New applications from fact-checking 
organizations must pass an initial screening 
process before applying to ensure they 
meet the minimum requirements — legal 
registration, financial transparency, 
methodology, editorial independence and a 
corrections policy. The IFCN staff completes 
this screening before assessment begins, as 
detailed above.
Between January and December 2024, the 
IFCN received a total of 180 new applicant 
requests. After the screening process, only 26 
met the minimum application requirements 
and were invited to complete the full 
application. Of the 26:
• Eight have not yet submitted the full 
application
• 16 are currently being assessed — four 
from Sub-Saharan Africa, one from 
MENA, four from Europe, one from 
North America, three from South 
America, two from South Asia and one 
from Asia Pacific in the assessment 
process
• Two applicants from Kenya and Liberia 
successfully completed the IFCN 
assessment process and are current 
signatories.
Renewal Applications
Between January and December 2024, the 
IFCN received 165 renewal applications. 
Of those, 108 completed the assessment 
process. Four were rejected due to sourcing or 
methodology issues.
Of the 135 applicants who completed the 
assessment process from January to December 
2024 and received a final determination, 
the advisory board voted on 125 of the 135 
applications with an average completion time 
of six months. The remaining 10 did not reach 
the advisory board due to organizational 
closures or failure to meet sourcing, 
publication or methodology requirements.

2024 Code Operations: 
Procedural and Technical 
Improvements 
In 2024, the IFCN updated the IFCN 
Code of Principles website, establishing 
a screening process for new applicants, 
improvements to the assessor onboarding 
process and implemented a two-year review 
process for signatories with five successful 
reviews. The purpose of this update was 
to attempt to streamline a process that had 
become too slow. In 2023, for example, the 
IFCN received 140 applications, with an 
average completion time of eight to 10 months. 
To streamline the IFCN assessment process 
and standards without compromising the 
quality or integrity, the IFCN made several 
workflow and platform improvements. These 
changes reduced the average assessment time 
from eight to 10 months to about six.
The screening process ensures applicants 
submit a full application only after they 
are deemed eligible. It also saves the IFCN 
staff time by reducing reviews of ineligible 
applications. The update improves application 
quality by giving constructive feedback to 
applicants who may not yet be ready and 
offering tips to new organizations to meet the 
minimum requirements to apply.
Also in 2024, signatories with five or more 
successful assessments and no serious 
complaints under the IFCN complaints 
policy were granted senior IFCN signatory 
status. These senior IFCN signatories enter 
into a two-year renewal process, which 
reduces annual application volume and 
improves assessor and applicant management. 
Improvements to the assessor recruitment 
and onboarding processes were also made 
in 2024 to ensure that the IFCN has enough 
qualified assessors with the academic 
background, media experience and language 
proficiency to verify whether an applicant 
meets the IFCN standards. 
To streamline the process for training new 
assessors, the IFCN and the Poynter Institute’s 
digital experience 
team created a new 
assessor screening 
and assignment 
process. Potential 
assessors must 
now submit a 
short biography 
that outlines their 
qualifications for 
a more intensive 
review by IFCN 
staff. 
A more robust 
onboarding process, which includes concrete 
examples and an increased level of nuance, 
ensures that the advisory board and readers 
have the necessary context to understand 
the assessment process, the applicant’s 
compliance with each criterion and provide 
important local context with relevant sources.
In 2024, the IFCN also worked with the 
Poynter digital experience team to overhaul 
the IFCN website to ensure accurate 
representation of a signatory’s status as 
active. In the previous iteration of the IFCN 
website, the signatory page relied on an 
automated internal dataset that was unable 
to accurately display a signatory’s status or 
take into account the 90-day grace period for 
reapplication, causing issues for signatories 
who relied on an active signatory status for 
their work. Applicants could submit multiple 
applications at once, resulting in unprocessed 
applications, preventing the signatory page 
from accurately reflecting if the applicant’s 
signatory status was active, in-renewal or 
expired. The updated IFCN signatory page 
now automatically updates the status of 
renewing applicants from active to in-renewal 
or expired based on the completion date 
of an application and without relying on a 
static dataset. This change has streamlined 
workflow, improved real-time reporting and 
removed the need for manual updates by the 
IFCN staff.
As part of the 2024 update, the IFCN and 
digital experience team improved the user 
experience for assessors, applicants and 
advisory board members. The previous 
website was difficult to navigate, and the 
application criteria were unclear. Users 
struggled to understand instructions or 
requirements and entered information in the 
wrong fields. This back and forth would result 
in inefficiencies and prolong the time needed 
to complete the assessment for both applicants 
and assessors.
The updates include a clearer delineation 
between the 31 criteria of the IFCN 
application, blocking out sections where 
applicant answers are not required, increased 
visibility between sections requiring amended 
answers from the applicant or assessor, 
and greater transparency in each criterion 
requirement and applicant and assessor 
answers for advisory board deliberation. 
Feedback has been positive. Users now 
navigate the application more easily, better 
understand what is required, and provide 
more constructive feedback. The advisory 
board also deliberates more efficiently, helping 
to streamline the assessment timeline.
After the new procedures and website were 
implemented, the IFCN worked with the 
advisory board to recruit and onboard eight 
new members.2
With the website and process updates, 
the IFCN advisory board also received 
onboarding training that explained the new 
process and instructions on how to navigate 
the website. 
This new process helps the IFCN 
gather feedback on the board’s priorities, 
shape future trainings, improve support 
for applicants and assessors, and foster 
transparent communication with the board.

4. Accountability 
and Transparency
Beyond advocating for transparency in the fact-checking community, the 
IFCN models these values in its Code of Principles through a structured 
accountability framework. This system includes a public complaints 
mechanism that enables stakeholders to address potential violations of the 
Code, reinforcing the organization’s commitment to consistent standards. 
Full details of this accountability process are available on the IFCN 
complaints page.
IFCN Complaints Process
In 2024, the IFCN updated its complaints 
process to limit frivolous complaints and 
more proactively inform signatories of alleged 
violations of the IFCN Code of Principles. 
This new process allows IFCN staff to review 
the merits of the complaints and categorize 
them as actionable, not actionable, or junk. 
The IFCN reviews actionable complaints and 
supporting evidence, allowing signatories to 
respond to legitimate complaints before their 
annual renewal. 
2024 Complaints
The IFCN complaints policy was revised 
during the 2024 website update. Previously, 
individuals submitted complaints through 
the IFCN website, which stored them in an 
internal database for staff review. IFCN served 
as a mediator between the complainant, the 
applicant and the assessor, who reviewed 
the complaint, typically during a regularly 
scheduled assessment. In reviewing this 
process, the IFCN saw that the complaints 
focused on single fact checks or unrelated 
issues, not violations of the Code. Hostile 
actors exploited the system to delay and 
prolong a signatory’s renewal process, since 
each complaint prompted a response. This led 
to capacity strains due to a large number of 
irrelevant or frivolous complaints.
To address these issues, the IFCN replaced 
the form with a dedicated complaints email 
inbox with specific requirements. Complaints 
must state which principle was violated, 
explain how it was violated, and include 
supporting evidence for review. The IFCN 
limits each person to three complaints 
per year to reduce abuse and frivolous 
submissions. Staff reviews the complaints, 
monitors trends and offers guidance to 
organizations or assessors. The process 
helps IFCN inform the public and provide 
more accurate information to address any 
grievances they may have about specific fact 
checks or third-party fact-checking programs.
Since launching the new process, the IFCN 
received 315 emails to the IFCN Complaints 
inbox in 2024. Of those, 279 were categorized 
as junk, 27 as not actionable and nine as 
actionable for staff review. “Junk” were 
incoherent or misunderstood the purpose of 
the IFCN complaints process. These emails 
had grievances concerning social media 
platforms, the loss of access to their individual 
accounts or did not contain any information. 
The “Not Actionable” category primarily 
concerned complaints with moderation 
policies on social media platforms, grievances 
with a single specific fact check that limited 
the user’s reach and profile, or were against 
signatory parent companies.
The nine complaints deemed actionable 
primarily focused on fair use, website 
transparency and accessibility, or claims 
of bias and nonpartisanship. The IFCN 
is currently in discussion with four 
organizations requesting clarifications 
regarding their nonpartisanship policies, the 
IFCN team has also assigned the complaints 
to an appropriate assessor to provide feedback. 
These complaints and responses will be 
reviewed and provided to another assessor 
and the advisory board during the signatory 
organization’s renewal application. The five 
other complaints concern less urgent issues 
of accessibility and clarity, which will be 
addressed when the organization reapplies to 
the IFCN Code of Principles.
In 2024, the IFCN processed one complaint 
alleging severe violations of the Code. 
After an extensive review of the evidence, 
consultation with assessors and the advisory 
board, and direct communication with the 
signatory, the IFCN found the evidence and 
complaint significant enough to warrant 
immediate revocation of signatory status. The 
organization was removed from the IFCN and 
is barred from engaging in IFCN activities or 
advertising itself as a member of the IFCN.
IFCN Operational Transparency 
In implementing our refined complaints 
process, the IFCN put in place requirements 
for input and final approval by the IFCN 
advisory board. This new complaints 
process allows the IFCN to better manage 
the flow of complaint emails, more readily 
identify legitimate complaints, guide readers 
towards better ways to raise concerns that 
fall outside the IFCN’s purview, and better 
monitor potential harassment campaigns and 
narratives. These trends and potential issues 
are shared with the IFCN harassment working 
group, communications working group and 
the advisory board as appropriate.

Training and Professional 
Development
Recognizing that education and continuous 
professional development are essential to 
maintaining high fact-checking standards, 
the IFCN began developing a comprehensive 
training program on the Code of Principles. 
The course is designed to help fact-checking 
journalists and organizations meet the Code’s 
requirements and strengthen their practices in 
both local and international contexts.
Set to launch at the end of 2025, the 
IFCN Code of Principles Course will be 
a self-paced, online program that guides 
organizations through each principle, the 
related criteria, and best practices for building 
and maintaining high-quality fact-checking 
operations. It will include real-world examples 
and common challenges fact-checkers face 
when verifying claims and evidence across 
different scenarios.
Each lesson will feature self-assessment 
quizzes to reinforce the IFCN’s fact-checking 
approach and current industry standards. 
The course will support applicants in 
understanding the Code’s requirements 
and help organizations identify areas of 
improvement in their processes and policies. 
Those who complete the IFCN course will not 
only know the content of the Code but will 
also be better prepared to meet its standards 
and improve their chances of successfully 
completing the IFCN assessment.
Community Engagement 
and Support
Strong community connections remain 
fundamental to the IFCN’s mission. Through 
targeted engagement initiatives and improved 
support systems, we’ve created more 
opportunities for collaboration and knowledge 
sharing. The IFCN has implemented dedicated 
webinars and individual consultations for 
new and renewal applicants to communicate 
any existing challenges in their applications, 
suggest site improvements and provide 
suggestions to strengthen the Code in new 
areas such as generative AI.
This framework allows applicants to have 
individualized and group consultations to 
address any specific questions, provide more 
relevant examples to specific challenging 
scenarios and ensure continuous compliance 
with the Code. This system changes improved 
communication channels for application status 
updates through an automated notification 
system for deadlines, application reminders, 
staff, assessor and advisory board action 
requirements, cutting the average timeline 
to complete the IFCN assessment process 
by two months when compared with 2023 
applications.
The IFCN significantly strengthened its governance framework in 2024 
by expanding the IFCN advisory board. This expansion brought in more 
diverse regional perspectives, with eight new members from Africa, Asia-
Pacific, Europe, Latin America, the Middle East and North America. This 
broader representation has proven crucial in addressing region-specific 
challenges and supporting the unique needs of fact-checking organizations 
worldwide. This expansion provided new opportunities to collaborate 
and discuss the growing challenges to press freedoms and the increase of 
targeted harassment campaigns against journalists; better communicate 
existing IFCN grant and funding opportunities to regional networks; and 
better develop strategies to improve the IFCN’s capacity to address ever-
changing information integrity challenges.

6. Next Steps 2025: 
Advancing the Future of 
Fact-Checking Journalism
Code of Principles Revision
The IFCN is undertaking a comprehensive research initiative to evaluate 
the current Code of Principles in light of emerging challenges in the 
information ecosystem. This initiative will gather community insights, 
analyze implementation feedback, and assess how the Code’s principles 
align with evolving verification needs. By examining developments 
in information dissemination, technological advances, and new 
organizational frameworks, the IFCN aims to build a foundation for 
potential future refinements.
will continue investing in our technical 
infrastructure to improve usability, determine 
potential automation features and ensure 
quality control among applicants and 
assessors. These improvements will focus 
on creating a more robust, stable and user-
friendly platform, automation of routine 
processes and notification systems, internal 
data analytics and reporting capabilities, 
and educational resources for fact-checking 
journalists on implementing the Code in their 
existing processes.
These improvements to the IFCN 
infrastructure and user experience will 
strengthen and grow the global fact-checking. 
Through these strategic initiatives, we aim 
to ensure that the Code continues to be the 
international standard for fact-checking 
journalism while adapting to new challenges 
in the international information ecosystem.
Continual Process 
Improvements
Building on the operational enhancements 
of 2024, the IFCN is committed to further 
strengthening our core processes to better 
serve the fact-checking community. Our 
continuous improvement strategy focuses on 
four key areas. We are standardizing assessor 
guidelines and training protocols to ensure 
consistent evaluations. We are refining the 
assessment framework through targeted 
automation of monitoring and management 
tasks. We continue implementing 
improvements based on comprehensive 
feedback mechanisms from the longstanding

developing accessible resources that help new

efficiency and quality across our certification 
ecosystem.

network initiatives. This exploratory process 
will engage fact-checking journalists and

groups focused on specific emerging 
challenges. To ensure broad stakeholder input, 
the IFCN will facilitate community feedback 
mechanisms to introduce the research process

potential updates that may strengthen the 
Code going forward.

Appendix 3: IFCN Revised 
Complaints Policy
To ensure our Code of Principles has 
integrity and usefulness, we will investigate 
complaints that critique a signatory for failing 
to uphold its standards. Readers who believe 
an organization has failed to adhere to the 
Code of Principles may submit a complaint to 
the International Fact-Checking Network for 
review.
Complaints may be sent via email to 
complaints@ifcn.org. They must include 
1. specific sections of the Code that have 
been violated, 2. supporting documentation, 
and 3. whether the complainant has 
contacted the fact-checking organization. For 
documentation purposes, complaints may not 
be made anonymously. 
IFCN Complaints 
Review Process
When a complaint is submitted, the IFCN will take 
the following steps:
1. IFCN staff reviews the complaint and 
specific evidence provided to determine if 
the complaint is clear and alleges specific 
violations of the Code of Principles.
• The complaint must be related to the 
standards in the Code of Principles. The 
IFCN will not investigate complaints that 
do not allege a violation of the Code.
• The IFCN will not accept complaints of 
individual fact checks or single topics 
unless a Code violation is involved. Such 
complaints should be pursued directly with 
the signatory.
• The IFCN does not investigate platform 
moderation policies or third-party fact-
checking programs unless a Code violation 
is involved. Complaints about third-party 
fact-checking programs should follow 
the guidance provided by the platform in 
question.
• The IFCN staff reaches out to the signatory 
for a response to the complaint. 
• The IFCN consults publicly available 
sources and/or conducts interviews for 
additional information.
• To prevent the use of the complaint 
process as a harassment tool, individuals 
are limited to three (3) complaints per 
calendar year. 
2. If a formal review is merited, the IFCN 
will assign an independent assessor to review 
complaints. The signatory will be informed 
of the review. The IFCN staff gives the 
information from the preliminary review 
to the assessor. The assessor conducts any 
additional needed investigation.
• The assessor provides a letter to the IFCN 
detailing the severity of the complaint and 
the local media environment. The assessor 
may request additional information to 
assist in their review.
• The signatory has 30 calendar days to 
formally respond to the IFCN regarding 
the complaint, provide evidence in their

defense and respond to any assessor 
questions. In urgent situations, the IFCN 
Director may require an expedited 
response.
3. The IFCN reviews all evidence and presents 
the evidence to the advisory board for review. 
In urgent cases, the IFCN director may ask the 
advisory board for expedited review.
• The IFCN may request additional 
information and evidence from the person 
who filed the complaint, the assessor or the 
signatory on behalf of the advisory board.
• The advisory board, in consultation with 
the IFCN director, will determine the 
consequences depending on the severity of 
the violation.
• Minor infractions: The IFCN director, 
advisory board and signatory will agree 
on an improvement process and a means 
of notifying the person who filed the 
complaint if the signatory has fallen short 
of the standards set in the code but (a) 
the infringement is real but not serious or 
systematic and (b) appears to result from a 
misinterpretation, not a wilful disregard, of 
the Code.
• Moderate Violations of the Code: The 
IFCN director and board may temporarily 
suspend signatory status if the signatory 
has violated one or more criteria of the 
Code and the matter is serious enough 
to require corrective action, requiring 
extensive corrective measures that take 
more than six months to correct and verify. 
The organization would need to reapply 
for signatory status once all measures have 
been completed.
• Serious Violations of the Code: 
The IFCN Director and board may 
withdraw signatory status if the signatory 
purposefully violates the ethical standards 
of the Code, deliberately attempts to 
conceal information or undermine the 
assessment process, or the IFCN and 
the signatory cannot resolve the issues 
outlined in the complaint.
Once the review is complete, the IFCN will 
notify the person who filed the complaint of 
the Advisory Board’s decision and actions 
based on their findings.
