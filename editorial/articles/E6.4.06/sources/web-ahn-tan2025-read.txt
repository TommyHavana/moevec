L23: ### Model and approach
L24: 
L25:   * Develops a new multi-country and multi-sector general equilibrium trade model to analyze the extent to which the diversification of sources of imports mitigates the impact of adverse trade shocks.
L26:   * Model incorporates trade network rigidities arising from frictions in goods, labor, and local factor markets.
L27:   * Emphasizes that countries cannot immediately reconfigure supply chains in response to shocks, creating a trade-off between resilience and efficiency.
L28: ### Key findings
L29: 
L30:   * Supply chain diversification can potentially improve resilience, at the cost of efficiency.
L31:   * Quantifying the resilience-efficiency trade-off suggests that diversifying the sources of targeted imports can enhance expected welfare when the probability of a large trade shock is sufficiently high.
L32:   * Targeted imports are characterized as:
L33:     * those more exposed to shocks,
L34:     * positioned upstream in the supply chain,
L35:     * and subject to greater rigidities.
L36: ### Policy implications and recommendations
L37: 
L38:   * Diversification policies should focus on targeted imports that are upstream, more exposed to shocks, and face greater market rigidities to maximize welfare gains relative to efficiency losses.
L39:   * The welfare benefits of diversification depend on the probability of large trade shocks; higher probabilities increase the attractiveness of diversification despite efficiency costs.
